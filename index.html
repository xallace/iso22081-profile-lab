Where ISO 2768 Falls Short

ISO 2768-2 (withdrawn 2021) has no profile tolerance, so free-form surfaces get no general tolerance, and its orientation tolerances take the longer leg of an angle as datum instead of a datum system. Step distances fall under the ± values of ISO 2768-1 (still valid), which ISO 14405-2 regards as ambiguous for dimensions that are not sizes. ISO 22081 replaces part 2 with a general surface profile ⌓ t | A | B | C and adds a general size specification for linear and angular sizes.

Migrating from ISO 2768-2 to ISO 22081

ISO 2768-2 was withdrawn in 2021; EN ISO 22081 supersedes EN 22768-2. ISO 2768-1 remains valid. Points marked “not verified” were not checked against the standard's text.

DO THIS IN ISO 22081
  • One Zone for All Untoleranced Surfaces: Cite ISO 22081 with a general ⌓ t | A | B | C so every plane, step, fillet, chamfer and free-form surface is held to its CAD TED geometry.
  • Separate Size from Geometry: Add a general size specification for untoleranced features of size (holes, slots, shafts, parallel faces): linear sizes (e.g. ±0.2 mm) and angular sizes.
  • Individual Specs for Critical Features: Indicate tight requirements (Ø25 H7, ⊥ 0.02 | A) directly on the feature. How they combine with the general specification is set in ISO 22081 §5.2 — read it before relying on “override” (not verified here).
LEGACY 2768-2 TRAPS TO AVOID
  • Don't Keep the K in ISO 2768-mK: With ISO 22081 it gives the drawing two general geometrical specifications that contradict each other. The m refers to ISO 2768-1, which is still valid.
  • Don't Drop the Datum System: Without | A | B | C | the general zone may be moved as a rigid body to best fit the part. Faces stay controlled relative to each other, but not relative to the functional datums. (Whether ISO 22081 makes a datum system mandatory: not verified here; §5.3 has rules for it.)
  • Don't Misread UZ: Per ISO 1101, ⌓ 0.4 UZ +0.2 means a 0.4 mm wide zone shifted +0.2 mm outward (band 0.00 .. +0.40 mm), not ±0.4 mm.
