Why ISO 2768-2 Was Withdrawn

Legacy ISO 2768-2 (withdrawn in 2021) left freeform surfaces uncontrolled, relied on the ambiguous “longer feature = datum” rule, and could not separate step distances from features of size. ISO 22081 replaces it with one crisp General Surface Profile ⌓ t | A | B | C plus an explicit General Size rule.

Migrating Cleanly from ISO 2768-2 to ISO 22081

ISO 2768-2 was officially withdrawn in 2021. Here is the exact GPS specification discipline required on modern engineering drawings.

DO THIS IN ISO 22081
  • One 3D Skin for All Untoleranced Surfaces: State ISO 22081 with a single ⌓ t | A | B | C callout so every plane, step, fillet, chamfer, and freeform surface inherits exact CAD TED limits.
  • Separate Size from Geometry: Add a General Linear Size tolerance (e.g., ±0.2 mm) specifically for untoleranced features of size (holes, slots, shafts, parallel plates).
  • Use Individual Specs for Critical Features: Tighter functional fits (Ø25 H7, ⊥ 0.02 | A) automatically override the general specification without needing exclusions.
LEGACY 2768-2 TRAPS TO AVOID
  • Never Mix ISO 2768-2 & ISO 22081: Leaving both in an old CAD title-block template creates conflicting legal interpretations in supplier audits.
  • Never Omit Datums on General ⌓: Writing ⌓ 0.4 without | A | B | C only controls surface form — step heights and wall angles can drift arbitrarily!
  • Don't Misread UZ: Under ISO 1101 / ISO 22081, ⌓ 0.4 UZ +0.2 means total width is 0.4 mm shifted +0.2 mm outward (band 0.00 .. +0.40 mm), NOT ±0.4 mm!
